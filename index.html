Is this real, or is it noise? Statistics and probability, the foundation under all of it.

Two tracks, one running brand. The 6-session leader track teaches executives, PMs, and marketers to read statistics without being fooled - mean vs median, what a confidence interval and a p-value actually claim, and the fallacies that sink smart rooms - with no code. The 10-session builder track takes practitioners from descriptive stats and probability, through distributions, random variables, sampling and the Central Limit Theorem, estimation and maximum likelihood, confidence intervals, hypothesis testing, chi-square and ANOVA, to correlation and regression - built from scratch in Python with numpy, scipy, and statsmodels. This is the foundation the experimentation and machine-learning courses assume you already have. Learn it on Lumen Skincare, then walk straight into A/B testing.

The leader track 🤝 · for execs, PMs & marketers · 6 x 45 min · no code

A statistic is only as good as the reading you give it. This track teaches you to interpret the numbers in any report - the center, the spread, the error bars, and the verdict - and to spot the fallacies that fool smart people, without opening a notebook. A friendly on-ramp for LinkedIn readers, too.

📊 Leader session 1 · start here

Reading the data

Mean, median, spread, and shape - and why the average is the most over-trusted number in business. Read a boxplot and a "typical customer" claim without being fooled.

▶ Start hereNo code 🎲 Leader session 2

Thinking in odds

Probability in plain language - independence, conditional probability, base rates and Bayes - and why a 99%-accurate test can still be wrong most times it fires.

Read the odds 🔔 Leader session 3

The bell and its cousins

The normal curve and the 68-95-99.7 rule, plus the binomial (counts of yes/no) and the Poisson (events per period) - what each shape tells you at a glance.

Distributions 🎯 Leader session 4

What a sample can say

Why a sample of thousands can speak for millions - sampling error, the Central Limit Theorem, and the margin-of-error intuition behind every poll and dashboard.

Sampling & CLT 📏 Leader session 5

The number and its error bars

Point vs interval estimates, and what a 95% confidence interval really means - plus the three things people wrongly think it means. Why "3.2% ± 0.5" beats "3.2%".

Confidence intervals ⚖️ Leader session 6

Real or noise?

The logic of a test, the p-value (defined correctly), significance vs importance, and the fallacy gauntlet - p-hacking, Simpson's paradox, correlation vs causation. Capstone: read a Lumen A/B result like an exec.

The builder track 🛠️ · for practitioners · 10 x 45 min · Python

For DA, DE, DS, and anyone who needs the statistics under machine learning and experimentation. One running brand - Lumen Skincare - carried across every session: describe it, simulate it, fit distributions to it, sample from it, estimate its parameters, and test hypotheses on it - all from scratch with numpy, scipy, and statsmodels.

📊 Builder session 1 · start here

Describe the data

Build the Lumen dataset once (seed 42), then center, spread, and shape in numpy/pandas - and why .describe() can quietly mislead you on skewed money.

▶ Start herePython 🎲 Builder session 2

Probability and simulation

Sample spaces, conditional probability and Bayes in code, and Monte Carlo simulation - watch the law of large numbers converge on Lumen's conversion rate.

Simulate it 🔢 Builder session 3

Random variables, E and Var

Discrete vs continuous random variables, expectation and variance, covariance, and the pmf/pdf/cdf trio - expected revenue per session, from first principles.

Expectation 🔔 Builder session 4

Distributions in scipy

Normal, binomial, Poisson (and uniform, exponential, lognormal) via scipy.stats - pdf/pmf/cdf/ppf, Q-Q plots, and fitting the right shape to Lumen's data.

scipy.stats 🎯 Builder session 5

Sampling and the CLT

Sampling distributions, standard error, and the Central Limit Theorem demonstrated by resampling - watch skewed order values average into a normal. Plus the bootstrap.

The money demo 🎛️ Builder session 6

Estimation and MLE

Estimators, bias and consistency, why we divide by N-1, and maximum likelihood - derive it, then let scipy find the parameter that makes Lumen's data most probable.

Maximum likelihood 📏 Builder session 7

Confidence intervals

z and t intervals for a mean, Wald and Wilson for a proportion, and the bootstrap CI - build the interval on Lumen's conversion rate that sets up the A/B test.

Error bars in code ⚖️ Builder session 8

Hypothesis testing

Null vs alternative, the test statistic, p-value, Type I/II error and power - the one and two-sample t-test and the two-proportion z-test. Is 3.6% really above 3.2%?

The verdict 🧮 Builder session 9

Chi-square and ANOVA

Chi-square goodness-of-fit and independence (channel vs convert), one-way ANOVA across product categories, and the nonparametric fallback when assumptions break.

More tests 📈 Builder session 10

Correlation, regression & fallacies

Pearson and Spearman, simple and multiple OLS, R-squared and residuals - then the fallacy capstone: p-hacking simulated, multiple comparisons corrected, Simpson's paradox in code.

One brand, both tracks. Every session works on Lumen Skincare - a synthetic $18M direct-to-consumer skincare label. Its numbers are the teaching examples: order values average ~$74 (median $63, right-skewed), checkout converts at ~3.2%, ~4,000 sessions a day, and prior spend correlates ~0.6 with order value. Leaders learn to read those numbers honestly; builders compute, simulate, and test them from scratch - and every figure lines up with the experimentation course, so this is the prequel that makes A/B testing click.
